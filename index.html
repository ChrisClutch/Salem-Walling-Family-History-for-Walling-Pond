A story told with care

History deserves evidence. Families deserve respect.

The Walling name appears in Oregon pioneer records, Salem industry, and documented accounts of military service.

Those connections matter, and each deserves proof. This independent tribute separates confirmed records, probable family links, and unresolved research. It is not an official Walling family website or a representation that the family endorses a proposal.

Evidence key

Verified Directly supported by a reliable source; transcriptions identified when the original has not been inspected.

Probable Consistent evidence, but a vital linkage or source remains to be checked.

Unconfirmed A lead, not an established family fact.

The place

What the family built. What Salem remembers.

Walling Sand & Gravel began in 1940 under Harold C. Walling, and its original processing location became the landmark known as Walling Pond. A quarry depression filled with groundwater and later attracted generations of anglers. Historic fishing access was a gift to the community; the site is now privately marketed for sale.

Owner identified by project sponsor: Jerry H. Walling Family Trust. This lead has not yet been matched to the recorded deed for each parcel. A 2026 City of Salem planning decision independently names Jerry H. Walling and Mike Walling as applicants through Walling Properties LLC at a different Lancaster Drive property; it does not establish pond title.

An invitation, not an assumption

Walling Veterans Cottages.

Could land that helped build Salem become a small neighborhood where veterans find home, community and a new beginning?

Concept: A Clutch Cottages-inspired cluster of accessible, single-level homes organized around a shared courtyard and gardens, with a common gathering room and a quiet place honoring military service. The pond would remain the landscape's centerpiece, subject to safety, environmental and public-access review.

A vision to discuss with the Jerry H. Walling Family Trust, veterans, neighbors and the City of Salem—not a claim of family approval or entitlement.

A thoughtful path forward

  1. Honor the family. Invite corrections, oral histories and archival photographs before any public dedication.
  2. Understand the land. Confirm deed, pond depth, quarry-slope stability, contamination, flood risk and usable upland.
  3. Study entitlements. Industrial General zoning does not automatically permit residential cottages; assess land-use amendments and environmental requirements.
  4. Shape the cluster. Test a modest group of zero-step cottages facing a common green, connected by walkable paths. Make parking, emergency access and accessibility work before fixing a home count.
  5. Design with veterans. Coordinate with qualified veteran housing and service partners; explore affordability, supportive services, communal programming and long-term stewardship.
  6. Preserve a landmark. Consider safe pond viewing, habitat, a memorial garden and controlled public access if studies make them feasible.
Important: 9.8 listed acres are not 9.8 proven buildable acres. No cottage count, layout, budget, entitlement, shoreline access or trust participation has been approved.
